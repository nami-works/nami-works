# NAMI Works — Brand Foundation

Stage 1 of the brand system: the strategic and verbal core everything else hangs off (voice/tone, site copy, ad hooks, decks). Source: Lucas's vision article (`.vision.md`), the project brief, and the shipped visual system (`site/BRAND.md`).

Working language of this doc is English (like `BRAND.md`); customer-facing surfaces are **PT-BR**. Brand voice is collective **"nós / a gente"** — founder-written, but never a single named individual.

## The name

**NAMI — Natural + Artificial Merged Intelligence.** Not AI bolted onto a business, and not humans using AI as a tool — the two *merged*: a company's real systems, policies and goals operated by AI that has been taught to work them.

## Point of view (the thing we actually believe)

There are two kinds of people working with AI today:

- **The chatters** — living in ChatGPT/Claude chat windows, copy-pasting between chats, docs, sheets and email to work a little faster.
- **The tweakers** — pushing AI into off-the-shelf integrations (Office, Figma, Canva, Adobe) to make life easier.

And inside the tweakers, a small group extracts most of the value that AI can offer today: they go *past* the off-the-shelf integrations and teach AI to operate **their own systems** — their ERP, their ecommerce platform, their custom Shopify apps. That is where Natural + Artificial Merged Intelligence actually happens. **That's where NAMI lives.**

This POV is the brand's center of gravity. It sorts the world (chatters → tweakers → operators), and NAMI stands at the far, high-value end.

## Positioning statement

For operators and founders who are already trying to push AI deeper into how their company actually runs, **NAMI Works** designs and runs AI-native operating systems that work *inside* your existing tools — ERP, ecommerce, custom apps — within your own policies and goals. Unlike chat-productivity tricks or generic off-the-shelf integrations, NAMI teaches AI to operate the business the way you would train a sharp new analyst: on your systems, to your standards.

## Who it's for

Leaders and operators of systems-heavy businesses — especially ecommerce/DTC brands and companies running ERP-driven B2B + B2C — who have felt the ceiling of chat-window AI and want it working behind the curtain, in the tools that run the company. **[DECIDE]** whether ecommerce/DTC is the hero audience (where the proof is) or a first beachhead under a broader "any systems-heavy company" positioning; the two service lines currently imply the broader read.

## The problem we solve

Most companies are stuck at the chatter/tweaker stage: AI helps individuals draft and think, but it never actually *operates* the business. The real work — pricing within commercial policy, keeping product pages consonant, turning first-party data into direction — still happens by hand, because off-the-shelf tools don't know your systems, your policies, or your goals.

## Value proposition

NAMI teaches AI to operate your actual stack the way a trained analyst would — inside your ERP, ecommerce platform and custom apps, within your discount thresholds, brand framework and objectives. Not faster chatting. Not another SaaS. Work that happens *in the systems that run the company*, supervised.

## Proof (real, kept anonymous in public)

Proven in-house on a real 8-figure-BRL DTC operation (**anonymous in all public material** — "uma marca de e-commerce de 8 dígitos"):

- **Consonant product pages at scale** — AI reads the hand-built PDP framework from Shopify (exact intent, tone, format) and replicates it seamlessly across the entire catalog.
- **Instagram from behind the curtain** — not from a screenshot or post URL, but through the API: every word said and every comment, read and turned into creative direction.
- **Proposals and invoices inside the ERP** — AI applies retail price points and per-client B2B commercial policy, then builds a full proposal or invoice within the maximum discount threshold, directly in the ERP.

## What makes us different

**There is no framework.** This is the sharpest, most ownable stance in the vision article, and it should stay central: NAMI is tailor-made to each company and use case. Like teaching an intern to use every SaaS your business runs, to accomplish *your* goals within *your* policies — not a template you buy. **[DECIDE]** how far to lean into this: the article's energy is peer/practitioner ("reach out so we can exchange learnings and evolve together"), while the company is now selling a service. Bespoke consulting is a business, but we need to choose how commercial vs. how peer the public voice is.

## Personality

The mark carries the personality; type, palette and copy stay restrained around it (per `BRAND.md`). In words, the brand is:

- **Practitioner, not pundit** — we've done it in a real operation; proof over promises.
- **Candid and anti-hype** — plain talk, no "AI startup" theater, comfortable puncturing hype ("Sorry, there is no framework").
- **Generous** — shares learnings, invites peers to evolve together rather than gatekeeping.
- **Precise** — specific about systems, policies, thresholds; concrete examples over adjectives.
- **Quietly confident, lightly playful** — the wink at the end of the article ("for the sake of Natural Intelligence…"); the humor lives in the mascot and the odd aside, never in loud copy.

We are not: hype-y, jargon-heavy, salesy, secretive, or self-serious.

## Messaging pillars

1. **Operating, not chatting.** AI that works inside your actual systems — ERP, ecommerce, custom apps — not another chat window.
2. **Tailor-made, not templated.** No framework. Taught to your policies and goals, like onboarding a sharp new analyst.
3. **Proven in the trenches.** Built and run by practitioners on a real 8-figure operation, not theorized on a slide.
4. **Supervised autonomy.** AI does the work; humans stay in the loop ("agentes de IA supervisionados").

These map to the two service lines — *Implementação de IA para gestão empresarial* and *Operação de e-commerce com IA* — which are the offering, while the pillars are the message beneath both.
