# Real estate — review playbook

## What this covers
Commercial `locação` / `sublocação`, serviced offices & coworking (Regus / IWG), shopping-mall
kiosks (`quiosque`), and storage/`depósito` space. Governed by Lei 8.245/1991 (Lei do Inquilinato)
plus the Civil Code. The recurring risk is **how you get OUT**: notice period, notice channel, and
early-exit penalty.

## Red-flag checklist (Brazil-tuned)
1. **Auto-renewal** — most serviced-office contracts auto-renew for periods equal to the current term
   until cancelled. Know the renewal mechanic.
2. **Notice period** — usually tiered by term. Regus/IWG pattern: month-to-month → 1 month (from the
   1st of a month); 3-month term → 2 months before term-end; >3 months → 3 months before term-end.
3. **Notice CHANNEL** — verify *how* notice must be given. Regus/IWG requires the **online account or
   app**; an email to the account manager does **not** count. This is the most common trap.
4. **Period boundaries** — terms typically run to the **last day of the month**; line the notice up
   before a month-end.
5. **Early-exit penalty** — leaving before term-end / without valid notice often = remaining fees to
   term-end + clawback of any discount/grace given. Time the exit to avoid it.
6. **Term & end date** — the binding notice number depends on the signed term; confirm it from the
   order form / portal, don't assume.
7. **Indexation (`reajuste`)** — anniversary adjustment, index (IGP-M / IPCA), frequency.
8. **Late-payment termination** — often immediate after >30 days overdue.
9. **Restoration fee** on exit; liability caps; deposit/`caução` return.

## Locked lessons
- Serviced-office (Regus/IWG) notice must go through the **portal/app** — email is not valid notice.
- The notice number (1/2/3 mo) hinges on the signed **term** — confirm term + end date before counting back.
- **Never cancel the old space before the new one is signed**; align old-end with new-start to avoid
  double-paying or triggering the early-exit penalty.
- Watch for name collisions: a Regus office "at Rio Sul" (Torre Rio Sul, Botafogo) is a different
  contract from a kiosk lease inside RioSul Shopping (Iguatemi). Don't conflate them.
- **JHSF mall-kiosk deals come as a TWIN pair** — a sublocação + a "fornecimento de serviço de vendas"
  (take-rate) contract, declared *indissociable* (rescind one → they can rescind the other). Review both
  together; the biggest combined risk is **cross-default** (forn. 9.6.x / sub. 11.7.x, extended to JHSF-defined
  "Related Parties"). Push to sever indissociability + limit cross-default to each contract's own obligations.
- **JHSF amends by adding lettered overrides to the Quadro Resumo** (e.g. "letra c) as cláusulas X, Y passam a
  vigorar com a redação abaixo"), not by editing body clauses — and the Quadro Resumo prevails. So to see what
  they conceded, read the Quadro Resumo amendment block, not the (often unchanged) body. Verify a deleted body
  clause is actually gone, not just overridden.
- **"Conditioned rent" trap:** a headline rent cut can be tied to a marketing counterpart (here: R$9k→R$5k
  conditioned on 2 Camila Coutinho visits + 2 posts/mo for the whole term + free image-rights license; breach
  reverts to R$9k). Price the counterpart's real cost before celebrating the discount — it's a CEO/brand call.
- **LGPD default is JHSF-favorable:** the lojista is cast as mere *operadora* (processor). Co-controladoria
  asks get rejected; note the residual liability exposure.
- Standing terms they leave untouched and rarely negotiate: **aluguel em dobro em dezembro**, holdover multa
  (R$/day amount), unilateral relocation right, unified-payment-platform mandatory adhesion.

## BR-law anchors
Lei 8.245/1991 (Lei do Inquilinato — locação não residencial) · Código Civil (subsidiary).
