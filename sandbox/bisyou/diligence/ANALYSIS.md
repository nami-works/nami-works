## TL;DR

The simple thesis ("cut the bloated influencer spend, keep the revenue, pocket the 80% margin") is **partly contradicted by the data**: revenue is **already collapsing (~-46% YoY) as the influencer engine is removed**, so we cannot assume the top line holds. Bisyou has been EBITDA-negative in 4 of its 5 years and is burning ~R$3M/yr in 2026.

BUT the **licensing structure caps our downside** and our **cost base would be radically lighter than theirs** (no debt, near-zero marginal overhead on shared infra, marketing converted to variable). On our cost base the brand breaks even at a far lower revenue than they need. Treated as a **capped-downside turnaround / call option powered by our owned audience (GE base + Camila) and the Flywheel CPA engine**, it can be accretive — but only if (a) the royalty is low single-digit % of **net** revenue with no minimum, and (b) 2026 monthly revenue is **stabilizing, not still free-falling**. That second point is the single fact that decides the deal, and it lives in the per-order file Ivan hasn't shared yet.

## 3. Re-testing the thesis against the data

**Thesis:** kill fixed influencer contracts → replace with revenue-tied micro-influencers (pay only on attributed revenue) → keep revenue, capture margin.

**What the data says:**
- The influencer/paid spend **was buying a large share of the revenue** (deck p77: top-10 influencers drove R$5.5M+ in tracked revenue; ROIs 2.1–6.7 but recent monthly ROIs decaying, several at 0–1.4). Pull it and revenue drops — which is exactly what 2025→2026 shows.
- So **we cannot model "revenue holds while spend is cut."** The honest model is "revenue resets to whatever owned + CPA channels sustain, and we size the cost base to be profitable at that lower level."
- The *replace* leg is genuinely our competency (Flywheel CPA + owned audience). The *retention* leg (how much revenue survives the cut) is the open risk and the data is not encouraging on its own.

## 4. Why it can still work — our cost base ≠ their cost base

The license is effectively a **call option on an 80%-margin brand, run on infrastructure we already pay for**. We strip out what's killing them:

| Cost line (their 2026 annualized) | Their burden | Our burden | Why |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| Financeiras / debt service | ~R$1.5M | **R$0** | No debt assumed (royalty structure) |
| Administrativas (contab. R$40k/mo, software R$69k/mo) | ~R$1.6M | ~R$0.3M | Runs on Omie/Shopify/team we already operate |
| Pessoal | ~R$1.1M | ~R$0.5–1.0M | We don't absorb staff; lean crew |
| Publicidade | ~R$3.2M | **variable** | CPA affiliates + owned audience, not fixed retainers |
| Despesas com vendas (frete/armaz/comissão) | ~R$1.2M | ~R$0.8–1.0M | Our 3PL / Rota Local stack |

**Plus the one real edge that Bisyou never had:** an **owned beauty audience** (GE Beauty base + Camila Coutinho / Garotas Estúpidas reach) to cross-sell skincare at near-zero CAC, instead of *renting* reach from influencers. That, not "cut costs," is the actual reason this could clear where they failed.

## 5. Break-even on our cost base (illustrative)

Assume: GM 80% of net rev; selling+card ~18%; CPA marketing ~18% of attributed rev; incremental fixed (lean team + marginal admin) ~R$1.25M/yr; royalty `r`% of net rev.

- Net contribution ≈ (80% − 18% − 18% − r) of net revenue ≈ (44% − r).
- At r = 8%: contribution ≈ 36% → **breakeven net revenue ≈ R$1.25M / 0.36 ≈ R$3.5M (~R$4.1M gross).**

Current 2026 run-rate is ~R$8M gross. **Revenue could fall by roughly half again from today's already-collapsed level and we'd still be ~EBITDA-neutral** — because the fixed base is gutted and marketing is variable. Above that, every incremental R$1 of net revenue drops ~36c to EBITDA. That is the margin of safety the licensing structure buys us.

## 6. Verdict

**Conditionally yes — as a capped-downside turnaround, not as the "free margin" story.** The brand is structurally unprofitable and shrinking, but the deal isolates us from its liabilities and our cost base + owned audience can be profitable at a fraction of their required revenue. It's a cheap call option on an 80%-margin asset, and a second tenant that proves the CPG Labs / Flywheel operating model on a brand we don't even own.

**The deal is good only if all of these hold:**
1. **Royalty ≤ ~5–8% of NET revenue, no fixed minimum.** A royalty on gross, or a minimum guarantee, kills it given the fragility.
2. **2026 monthly revenue is stabilizing, not still falling.** ← the deciding fact. Need Ivan's per-order file: monthly trend + **repeat-purchase rate / % organic**. If revenue is still free-falling and repeat is low, it's terminal decline and we pass.
3. **License term long enough** (and cheap enough to exit) to prove the CPA + owned-audience model before any purchase obligation.
4. **Working capital ~R$1M** to fund stock (Dec/25 position: SP R$611k + MG R$353k; giro 3.0).
5. **Honest accounting of opportunity cost** — 3-person team; this pulls leadership attention off GE Beauty.

**Negotiation levers / open diligence:**
- Royalty %, base (gross vs net), and any minimum — **the single biggest unknown; the road-show deck does not contain it.**
- Cap table is **76% Disruptive Participações S/A** (financial holder) + 12% Ivan + 12% Carolina (deck p82). A PE-type holder licensing out operations is a mild **negative-selection signal** — they couldn't fund/fix it. Price the option accordingly.
- Agency: R$60k/mo min media + R$6.5k service to Nov/2026 with rescission penalty — inherit vs pay penalty.
- DPSP B2B channel effectively dead (R$1.7M→R$584k→~0). Don't pay for it; treat any revival as upside.
- ANVISA registrations / trademark / litigation — confirm clean (already on Lucas's list).
