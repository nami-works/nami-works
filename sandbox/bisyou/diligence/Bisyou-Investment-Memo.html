1. Recommendation at a glance

CONDITIONAL GO  Pursue to a term sheet, inside the gates below. The thesis is narrower than v1: with no demand synergy (no cross-sell, no Camila), the entire case is an operational cost-arbitrage — take Bisyou's own revenue and run it on a far lighter cost base than the current owners can. The downside is genuinely capped; the upside is real but modest and hinges on one thing: whether we can acquire Bisyou's customers efficiently on its own brand.

Big picture. Bisyou loses ~R$3M/yr at a run-rate and has been profitable in only one of five years — the cost of buying its customers always ate its excellent (~80%) margin. But the licensing structure hands us the operation with no debt, no labour, no legacy payables, and (because Boniteca finances the stock) no working-capital outlay. On our cost base — no debt service, lean shared overhead, marketing as variable CPA instead of fixed influencer retainers — the same revenue flips to roughly R$1M of EBITDA. The brand's own-site business is durable (32% of orders repeat), so there is a real asset underneath. The bet is no longer "use GE's audience"; it is "run lean and acquire with discipline where the incumbents couldn't."

The gates (all must hold, or we pass):

  1. Royalty ≤ ~5–8% of NET revenue, no fixed minimum. On gross, or with a minimum, the math breaks.
  2. We can run standalone acquisition at ≤ ~35% of net revenue. This is the new crux: with no cross-sell crutch, the whole upside depends on marketing efficiency. Break-even is ~39%; the owners ran 48% (recent) and 32% (their one good year). Prove it with a small paid test before committing.
  3. May/June own-site revenue is stabilising, not still sliding. We do not have these two months yet; this is a forward-looking diligence gate.
  4. License term long enough and cheap enough to exit to prove the model pre-purchase; plus clean ANVISA, trademark, no live litigation.
  5. We cap the leadership attention committed. The real cost is bandwidth pulled off GE Beauty, not cash.

2. What is actually on the table

Big picture. A brand-licensing and operational takeover, not an acquisition. We run Bisyou and pay a royalty, with an option to buy later (~year five). We take the operation; we do not take the balance sheet. The two brands stay independent on the demand side — only the operational machine is shared.

Structure (clean handover)

  • No liabilities transferred: no labour liabilities, no bank debt, none of the ~R$1.9M legacy payables. Pre-closing items stay with the owners.
  • Supply unchanged: made by Boniteca (same manufacturer as GE). Boniteca fee is a 10%→5% regressive success fee, paid only after sale.
  • No working-capital outlay (corrected): Boniteca finances the inventory; we pay on consignment-style terms, ~M+2 after the sale (e.g. June sales paid the 5th business day of August). We pay only after the cash is in. There is no ~R$1M stock pre-funding.
  • Marketing today: influencer contracts already cancelled; Meta/Google agency runs to Nov-2026 (~R$60k/mo media + R$6.5k service, break fee on early exit).

Who we are dealing with

Cap table: Disruptive Participações S/A 76%, Ivan Pinheiro 12%, Carolina Viudes 12%. A financial holder controlling 76% and licensing out operations is a mild negative-selection signal — they could not fund or fix it. That is why the royalty and the purchase strike must be priced as the call option this is.

7. The risks, and where the trap lives

1. Standalone acquisition efficiency (the new #1). With no cross-sell, everything rides on buying Bisyou's customers at ≤ ~35% of net. The incumbents — who knew the brand — ran 48%. If we can't beat that materially, the EBITDA evaporates. Mitigant: a paid test before we commit.
2. The own-site base keeps sliding / is spend-dependent. Mar (R$909k)→Apr (R$597k) dipped, and per §4 we can't prove Shopify is independent of the old bloat. May/June is the tell.
3. The royalty is set wrong. Anything on gross, or with a minimum, transfers the fragility to us. Still unstated — we hold the pen.
4. Attention cost. A three-person team; every hour on Bisyou is an hour off GE Beauty.
5. Product / inventory risk (not working capital). Boniteca carries the stock, so no cash outlay — but some SKUs are liquids/glass (água micelar, sabonete) with breakage and shelf-life exposure, and demand on slow SKUs is uncertain.

8. Cost-to-serve: freight, corrected

Your C7 was right and I've corrected it. The "59% shipped free / R$6.52" figure is what Bisyou charged its customers (a revenue/pricing choice), not its cost to ship. I conflated the two and wrongly claimed Unilog would "double" their cost. Their actual carrier (J&T/R3) cost is not in the data, so that comparison cannot be made yet.

What we can compute is our own cost-to-ship. We priced every one of the 44,969 reconstructed orders against GE's Unilog B2C table (origin: Serra/ES filial), by destination and basket:

Full per-order calculation, summary, and the rate-table assumptions are in Bisyou-Unilog-Freight-Cost.xlsx. UF-level representative rates; exact CEP→GEOCOM-zone mapping would refine within a UF. No weight data in the file, so cubed weight is inferred from basket size (Southeast bands are near-flat 0.25–2 kg, so this barely moves the dominant ~57% SE volume).

So the freight conclusion is now: our cost-to-ship is ~R$26/order; whether that is cheaper or dearer than what Bisyou pays J&T/R3 today is open until we see one month of their carrier invoices. Don't assume a saving or a loss. The real fulfilment synergy is warehousing/pick-pack consolidation; the carrier itself should be chosen on the actual quote.

9. Deal terms and negotiation levers

  • Royalty: cap at single digits of NET, no minimum; tie step-ups to revenue we actually rebuild.
  • Purchase option: fix the strike now and low — we rebuild the equity during the licence; don't pay twice for our own work.
  • Marketing-efficiency proof: structure a short paid test (or an earn-in) so we validate the ≤35%-of-net acquisition before deep commitment.
  • Agency: decide inherit-vs-break-fee on the Meta/Google contract (to Nov-2026); don't commit to its R$60k/mo minimum beyond our plan.
  • Dead channels are upside, not value: don't pay for DPSP/B2B (dead) or the TikTok peak. Price the durable base only.
  • Clean reps: ANVISA active with dates, trademark uncontested, no live litigation, all access at closing.

10. Verdict and next steps

CONDITIONAL GO  The verdict survives your corrections, but the reasoning changed: the downside is lower than v1 (no working capital, no debt, clean licence), while the upside is narrower (no demand synergy — pure operational arbitrage). It holds because the cost structure tolerates a near-halving of revenue or marketing far less efficient than the incumbents' best. It fails if the royalty is greedy, the own-site base is still in free-fall, or we cannot prove standalone acquisition under ~35% of net.
