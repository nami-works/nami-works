# Variant B — Mid-morning insert shot — candidate scenarios

**Purpose.** Insert a 4th shot between current Shot 1 (7:50 AM bathroom application) and Shot 2 (3:15 PM cafe storm) of the primer-liso-intacto reel. The insert must occupy the **late-morning / mid-morning window (9:30 AM - 12:00 PM)** — the gap where Marcela transitions from "primer applied, ritual sealed" to "out in the world, storm incoming." It carries Hour ~2-4 of the 24-hour shield arc: hair is locked, the day is in motion, the test has not yet peaked.

**Brand-grammar constraints (locked across all candidates).**
- Faceless + hair-out (no hair, no face in frame).
- Held-witness OR single-hand-entry; bottle is the silent constant.
- Eye-level macro, conservative motion, no copy on screen.
- Cardigan-sleeved wrist (oatmeal/cream knit per persona_dims afternoon wardrobe) since this is the daytime out-of-home block — same sleeve continuity as Shot 2, which is intentional (one outfit, three moments).
- Bottle on a real surface, real contact shadow, real specular at base.
- Marcela never opens / activates the bottle here — `passo_a_passo` step 3 ("reaplique pequenas quantidades nos dias seguintes para manter o alinhamento") frames the bottle as a tool she CARRIES with her for potential refresh, not one she uses publicly mid-morning.

## Section 1 — Candidate scenarios

### Candidate A — The tote-bag handoff at the elevator landing

- **Time of day.** 9:35 AM.
- **Setting.** Marcela's apartment building elevator landing. The bottle sits on the slim console table by the elevator call button — a polished travertine slab with a single brass key tray. She set the bottle down for two seconds while shifting her tote from one shoulder to the other before the elevator arrives. Cool indirect light from the corridor's skylight.
- **Motion pattern.** Pattern B (frame-entry) — the cardigan-sleeved hand returns to lift the bottle back off the travertine and into her tote (which is off-frame).
- **Hand / object motion beat.** At 2.8s her cardigan sleeve enters from the right, slim manicured fingers close around the bottle's body, lift cleanly upward and out of frame. By 4.5s only the travertine + brass key tray remain. The bottle's contact shadow lingers a half-beat then is gone with it.
- **What it communicates.** Hour 2 of the shield: she's PORTABLE — the primer travels with her because the brand promise lives in her bag. The bottle is leaving the home, going into the day.
- **Continuity to shots 1 + 2.** Bridges the bathroom (Shot 1) to the cafe (Shot 2) by showing the literal handoff — bottle leaves the apartment via her tote, arrives later on the cafe table. The viewer reads the through-line: same bottle, three places.
- **Photographically distinct.** Travertine is a new surface texture (vs Shot 1 marble, Shot 2 dark wood, Shot 3 concrete). The brass key tray is a unique prop ecosystem. Corridor skylight is cool indirect — distinct from Shot 1's bathroom warm tungsten and Shot 2's storm-window grey.
- **Risk / why this might be wrong.** Slightly narrative-heavy (bottle being CARRIED rather than at rest); risks reading as a transport shot rather than a held-witness moment. Also forces a new location anchor (elevator landing) that the prior screenplay did not establish.

### Candidate B — The office desk corner, between meetings

- **Time of day.** 10:50 AM.
- **Setting.** Marcela's open-plan office desk corner — a pale oak veneer surface. The bottle sits beside a closed black leather notebook, a black ballpoint resting diagonally across the notebook, and a small ceramic mug of cold green tea (steam already gone, she made it 40 minutes ago). Soft north-facing window light from the left, no direct sun.
- **Motion pattern.** Pattern A (held witness) with one micro-beat: the cardigan-sleeved hand enters from the right and turns the closed notebook a quarter-turn (re-aligning her workspace before the next meeting), then withdraws. The bottle is never touched.
- **Hand / object motion beat.** At 3.1s her sleeve enters; fingers grip the notebook's spine, rotate it ~30° to square it with the desk edge, exit. By 4.6s desk is settled. Bottle witnesses a moment of micro-rituality.
- **What it communicates.** Hour 3 of the shield: she's at work, hair is locked in, and her workspace is calm because SHE is calm — the primer did its morning job. The cold green tea + closed notebook signal a brief between-meetings exhale.
- **Continuity to shots 1 + 2.** Connects the bathroom ritual (Shot 1's methodical dryer-then-cap) to the cafe pause (Shot 2's cardigan-sleeve cup-lift) by showing a THIRD micro-ritual of self-ordering — same character, three small acts of control across the day.
- **Photographically distinct.** Pale oak veneer is a new surface (vs marble, dark wood, concrete). Office prop ecosystem (notebook, pen, ceramic mug) is entirely new to the reel. North-facing diffuse light reads colder than the bathroom and warmer than the cafe storm — fills the gap.
- **Risk / why this might be wrong.** "Office desk" is the most generic of the four — risks reading as stock B-roll. Also requires a new location-anchor entry in Step 3.45 that the screenplay's current Sao Paulo neighborhood arc does not name.

### Candidate C — The cafe counter at the bakery pickup, mid-morning errand

- **Time of day.** 11:20 AM.
- **Setting.** The marble pickup counter of a neighborhood padaria. The bottle sits on the cool white marble beside a small white paper bag (folded shut, neighborhood bakery logo embossed on the side) and a R$5 note folded under the bag's edge. Warm overhead pendant light from the right (off-frame), the morning ambient sun spilling in faintly from the street-facing window at the back of frame.
- **Motion pattern.** Pattern B (frame-entry) — the white paper bag is the moving element: it has just been set down by an off-frame baker's hand (which is GONE by the time the clip opens). The micro-motion is the bag settling a half-millimeter as a pastry inside resettles its weight.
- **Hand / object motion beat.** No hand enters the frame for this candidate. At 1.4s the bag micro-settles (a small visible shift of the folded paper top). At 3.5s a tiny shadow flickers across the marble from a pedestrian passing the window behind. Bottle never moves.
- **What it communicates.** Hour 3.5 of the shield: she's out running a normal SP-paulistana errand — bakery, padaria, the everyday ritual of a Brazilian morning. The primer is with her because it always is. The shield is doing its silent work while she does her real life.
- **Continuity to shots 1 + 2.** The marble surface visually rhymes with Shot 1's marble counter — same material, different context (private → public). Bridges the bathroom (private marble) to the cafe (public dark wood) by showing a public marble in between.
- **Photographically distinct.** Same material as Shot 1 (marble) but read as a different surface because of context, light direction, and prop ecosystem (paper bag, money). Pendant light from off-frame right is a new lighting direction.
- **Risk / why this might be wrong.** Marble-on-marble continuity with Shot 1 may visually echo too closely; some viewers may read it as the same room. Also: NO hand-enters is unusual for the reel's grammar — three of the four shots would have a hand-entry beat, this one would not. May feel flat next to the others.

### Candidate D — The car center console at a red light on Av. Rebouças

- **Time of day.** 9:55 AM.
- **Setting.** The center console of Marcela's car at a red light. The bottle is wedged in the cup-holder well, leaning slightly against the right wall of the well. Her phone (face down, slim leather case) sits in the adjacent holder. Through the windshield, mid-morning Sao Paulo: the back of the next car, a faint heat-shimmer off its trunk, building facades blurred in soft bokeh. Direct mid-morning sun from the left (driver's window), strong but not harsh.
- **Motion pattern.** Pattern A (held witness) — bottle and console locked. Background motion only: the heat-shimmer drifts, the bokeh of buildings holds, the light shifts by a tone as a cloud passes.
- **Hand / object motion beat.** No hand. At 4.0s the light turns (visible through windshield as the brake lights of the car ahead snap off — a quick color shift in the bokeh). The bottle witnesses the city about to move.
- **What it communicates.** Hour 2 of the shield: she's in transit, the city is in motion around her, but inside the car the bottle is calm — the primer's umbrella effect is the calmness she's bringing with her into the day.
- **Continuity to shots 1 + 2.** Bridges Shot 1 (interior stillness, bathroom) to Shot 2 (interior stillness, cafe by storm window) by introducing a THIRD interior framed against an exterior — same archetype, escalating environmental drama (bathroom = no exterior, car = mid-morning city, cafe = storm).
- **Photographically distinct.** Car interior is a fully new environment in the reel. The cup-holder well is a unique surface — fabric-textured rubberized plastic, very different from marble/wood/concrete. Through-windshield bokeh is a new lighting layer.
- **Risk / why this might be wrong.** Cup-holder framing is awkward at eye-level macro — the bottle would be partially below the console line, requiring an unusual camera angle that breaks the reel's established eye-level horizontal grammar. Also: the heat-shimmer outside risks reading as "summer heat sensation" and stealing emotional space from Shot 2's storm sensation.

## Section 2 — Recommendation

**Candidate B (office desk corner, 10:50 AM) is the strongest insert.** It is the lowest-risk addition to the existing screenplay because it preserves the reel's established grammar (eye-level macro, single cardigan-sleeve hand-entry, held-witness bottle, real surface contact) while doing the most narrative work per second: the cold green tea + closed notebook reads as Hour 3 of a real workday, the micro-ritual of squaring the notebook rhymes with Shot 1's dryer-placement and Shot 2's cup-lift to make the reel feel like four moments of one calm woman's quiet ordering of her world, and the pale oak veneer + north-window light fill genuine photographic gaps between Shot 1's warm marble and Shot 2's cold storm-grey. Candidate A is more narratively explicit but risks reading as transport rather than witness. Candidates C and D introduce surface- or framing-continuity risks that would force regen cycles.
