# Promotions as a system (not a panic button)

**Source:** Nik Sharma, "Promotions 101: How to run offers without being a discount
brand" (2026-02-22, growth digest). **Provenance:** external best-practice. **Why it's
here:** it operationalizes GE's value-add-over-discount guardrail into a concrete toolkit
+ calendar.

## The core mistake
Reactive sales (revenue dips → "run a sale") spike revenue for 48h, then baseline
returns — but you've **trained customers to wait for the next deal** and shredded margin.
Durable brands treat promotions as a *system*: a calendar, a structure, a reason behind
every offer, and guardrails. Promote on a plan (intentional generosity), never as a
panic button (desperation).

## Not all promotions are discounts — the 12-tool spectrum
Most brands only use 2-3 (usually % off). The best tools don't cut price. Ordered here
roughly premium-safe → discount-y:

- **Gift with purchase (GWP)** — the premium brand's best friend. Adds value without
  cutting price; perceived value >> your cost; doubles as a way to seed/trial a new SKU.
- **Bundle pricing** — "Starter Kit / Complete System" priced 15-20% under à-la-carte.
  Discount built into the *structure*, not slapped on as a code. Raises AOV, not "sale"-y.
- **Free-shipping threshold** — a promo disguised as policy ("free over R$X") + a cart
  progress bar ("R$18 away from free shipping") to lift AOV.
- **VIP / loyalty-only access** — price stays; *access* is the reward ("members get first
  access to the drop"). Urgency without discounting.
- **Early access / pre-sale** — email/SMS buys 24-48h before public. The access is the value.
- **Subscribe & save** — "save 15% when you subscribe" reads as a pricing tier, not a sale,
  and locks in LTV. (A sleeper.)
- **Tiered spend-to-save** — "spend R$100 save 10%, R$150 save 15%…" grows cart size.
- **Dollar-off / threshold** — better than % at lower AOV ("R$10 off" > "8% off"); "spend
  R$100 get R$20 off" controls the discount + nudges AOV.
- **BOGO / Buy X Get Y** — great for consumables; "buy 2 get 1" feels generous (free) vs a
  number; moves volume + introduces variants.
- **% off** — the default; scales with AOV but trains full-price avoidance if overused.
  Safer as an *always-on creator code* (e.g. Ridge's 10% influencer codes) — a soft
  discount tied to a *relationship*, not a promotional event.
- **Flash sale** — only 2-3×/year, scarcity must be real; monthly "flash sales" are just
  your price with extra steps.
- **Mystery / spin-to-win** — converts but can read cheap; **skip for premium positioning.**

Before any promo, scan this list and pick the format that fits the goal — usually a
non-price tool for a premium brand.

## Cadence framework
- **4-6 major promotional moments/year** (real "events"): New Year, a Mother's/Father's/
  Valentine's moment, a summer moment if relevant, Back-to-School if relevant, Black
  Friday/Cyber, Holiday/EOY. Pick the ones that fit the audience — not all apply.
- **Monthly "soft promotions" with no discount:** new drops, limited editions, bundle
  launches, creator collabs, content series. Keeps the brand active/interesting without
  touching price. (The Feastables / Liquid Death playbook: "promotions" = new things to
  talk about, not new prices.)
- **Quarterly loyalty moments:** VIP access, subscriber appreciation, referral pushes.
  Reward best customers without discounting for everyone.

## When NOT to promote
- **Right before a product launch** — discounting cannibalizes the launch.
- **Reactively, to plug a soft week** — that's the death-spiral that trains wait-behavior.

## Applying it (premium DTC brand)
Default to the non-price tools (GWP, bundles, free-ship threshold, VIP/early access,
subscribe & save); keep % off as an always-on creator-code relationship, not an event; run
offers off a calendar; never a panic sale; skip spin-to-win for premium positioning.
(Brand-agnostic. A given brand's current thresholds, codes, and calendar live in that
brand's operational config, not here.)
