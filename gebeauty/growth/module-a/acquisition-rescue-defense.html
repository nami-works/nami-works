01 · The decision on the table

Launch two campaigns built on the products the data says actually retain

We spend to acquire new customers, but the business only grows if they come back. So the question under both campaigns is simple: which products actually make someone come back? We are deciding, this week: (A) a new-customer acquisition offer on the landing page and checkout, and (B) a single rescue email to the one-time buyers who never tried the products that do. First, the data settles what a "hero" actually is.

Neither campaign is judged on first-order AOV or margin (CGO guardrail 6). The metric is hero-trial rate and, through it, repeat rate (base 14.4% in this universe / 15.8% all-customer).

04 · Measurement hygiene

Isolating the acquisition-cohort AOV outliers

We are not claiming AOV is falling. This is what happens when a tripwire cohort is read against the blended baseline, and why guardrail 6 now segregates it.

Giveaway order vs normal order (last 30 days)

The free-travel-size offer is a bursty, currently-live campaign: 744 orders in ~3 weeks, 16% of orders.

Blended in, the tripwire drags reported AOV down 75%. Isolate it and the "AOV problem" is an artifact. The real risk is the opposite: trial that never converts to a 2nd purchase, which the hero data now lets us measure directly.

07 · The plan

Isolate first, then rescue, then acquire

1. Tag & isolate (first move)A dedicated discount-code family + order tag + customer segment per campaign, plus UTMs, before any order lands. Both cohorts stay out of the blended baseline (guardrail 6). Resolves attribution and the LP↔checkout de-dup (claimed on LP → suppressed at checkout by tag).
2. Rescue email (single send)To ~17,130 (18,781 emailable minus <30-day recency). Yes → buy Shampoo+Máscara, get Shampoo a seco free (the wash routine). No → 40% off next order.
3. Acquisition offer → LP + checkout50% off a wash hero (001/002) + travel of a second, built on the wash core, not the viral primer — because the primer is the anti-hero. Lighter-touch at checkout (primer-led R$175 carts do not want friction).
4. Measure downstreamSuccess = hero-trial rate and 2nd-purchase rate vs a holdout, read at day 30/60/90. Not first-order AOV.
First move: stand up the tagging + discount codes (avoiding the live-name collisions the analyst flagged). Nothing sends or spends until then.
08 · Risks and what could kill it

Honest caveats

  • We acquire on the anti-hero. Primer Cachos is the viral, cheapest traffic and the worst retainer. Building the acquisition offer on the wash core (not the primer) may lower front-end conversion even as it lifts retention. This is the real test, and the reason for the holdout.
  • Acquisition offer reads as clearance. 50% off + a free travel size is a 65% perceived discount. Mitigate by leading with the gift, not the percentage.
  • Value-add is not a silver bullet on retention. It beats broad discounting but only ties a clean first-order discount (D12). The stronger case for the gift is conversion (redemptions) and brand, not a retention miracle.
  • One email, one shot. The <30-day exclusion protects against asking before the customer can judge their first product.
  • Provisional freight + a fresh 008 read. Margins use a 15% freight placeholder; Shampoo a seco (008) as a hero is a newer signal than 001/002 — worth a maturity re-check in a cycle.
  • Measurement discipline is the whole game. Without cohort isolation we will misread AOV and conversion exactly as before this review.
09 · The ask

Six decisions to approve

A · Adopt the data-defined heroes rec: yes
Heroes = 001 Shampoo + 002 Máscara + 008 Shampoo a seco (the wash core); leave-in 003 is a neutral retainer, not a hero. This definition drives both offers. Rescue YES gift = Shampoo a seco 008 (completes the 25%-repeat routine, R$86 contribution, 27% perceived discount).
B · Build acquisition on the wash core, not the primer rec: wash core + hold a primer control
Point the 50%-off + travel offer at 001/002, since the primer does not retain. Risk: lower front-end conversion. Keep a primer-offer control arm to measure the trade.
C · Acquisition A/B variable rec: new offer vs old-giveaway holdout
Drop the "must add an item" test (mooted by the buy-2 structure); instead test the new offer against a holdout on the pure travel-size hook, to isolate its effect on trial and 2nd purchase.
D · Broaden the rescue to all non-hero one-time buyers rec: yes, ~17,130
19,206 cohort (18,781 emailable) minus <30-day recency → ~17,130. Suppress anyone in an active win-back flow. Confirm the single send.
E · Checkout upsell weight rec: lighter-touch
Lighter nudge vs mirroring the LP offer. Abandoned carts are primer-led at R$175; friction risks conversion.
F · Redemption limits + the send/spend go
Single-use per customer on all codes. The email send and any media spend need explicit approval before they go live. (Done already: the two abuse codes TESTE99OFF and BHSFREESELIA are deactivated.)
